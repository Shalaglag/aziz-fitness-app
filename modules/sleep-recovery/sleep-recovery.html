Sleep and recovery plan

On a cut, sleep decides how much of the weight you lose is fat and how much is muscle. This page turns your bedtime into an evening plan and gives you a quick morning check for how hard to train.

Sleep target 7.5–9 h Floor 7 h Room 18–21 °C Wake time ±1 h, every day

Your evening

11:30 pm is an example. Set your usual bedtime for this night and the page remembers it for each day.

    Morning check

    Ten seconds after your weigh-in. It tells you whether to push for a PR, match last time, or go easy.

    Why it matters on a cut

    • In a well-known study, dieters on the same deficit who slept 5.5 hours instead of 8.5 lost about the same weight, but much less of it was fat and more of it was muscle.
    • Short sleep raises hunger and cravings, which makes 2,000 kcal feel harder than it is.
    • Strength is usually the first thing to slip after bad nights, and holding your loads is the point of this cut.

    Your day, set up for sleep

    • Training at 3:30 pm suits sleep well. Just keep pre-workout caffeine before the cutoff above.
    • Meal 2 at 8–9 pm is fine. Finish about 2 hours before bed. Nuts or Pro Mud Cake with Meal 2 are fine too.
    • Front-load water: most of your 3–4 L by early evening, then sips, so you don't wake up to pee.
    • Keep magnesium in the evening. It may help a little; you won't feel knocked out by it.

    Bedroom

    • Cool: AC around 18–21 °C. A cool room helps you fall asleep and stay asleep.
    • Dark: blackout curtains or an eye mask, and cover any LEDs.
    • Quiet: earplugs, or steady fan or AC noise if the street is loud.
    • Charge your phone away from the bed if you tend to scroll.

    Friday and Saturday

    • Keep your wake time within an hour of your weekday time, Friday included.
    • You train Saturday at 10 am after the 8:00–8:30 egg breakfast, so Friday night is the one that matters most.
    • Friday's salty Cajun shrimp can raise Saturday's weigh-in, and a short night adds to that. Neither is fat.

    Recovery in training

    • Take the rest days in your rotation. A walk is fine if you want to move.
    • Keep the 30 minute treadmill easy (4.0 km/h, incline 3). It shouldn't turn into hard cardio.
    • Stop sets 1–2 reps short of failure most of the time. Grinding past that on every set costs more recovery than it gives back on a cut.
    • Your cut rule: if a main lift drops for 2+ sessions in a row, check sleep first, then pre-workout food, then volume. Don't cut calories for it.
    • If several main lifts drop at once, or after 6–8 hard weeks, take a deload week: same loads, about half the sets.

    Sleep and the scale

    • Weigh in after waking and using the toilet, before food or water.
    • A bad night can hold extra water and bump the scale. Judge by the 7-day average.

    Naps

    • A 15–20 minute nap before about 1:30 pm is fine and can help the afternoon session.
    • Skip long or late naps if you struggle to fall asleep at night.

    When to see a doctor

    Loud snoring, choking or gasping at night, morning headaches, or daytime sleepiness despite 7–8 hours can be signs of sleep apnea. It's common at higher body weight and worth getting checked. Losing weight often helps.
