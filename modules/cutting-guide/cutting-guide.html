The short version

  1. Keep the weights heavy. The load on the bar is the signal that tells your body to keep muscle.
  2. Keep your sets as they are. Don't add volume to burn calories; the deficit does that.
  3. Hit 160–170 g protein every day, including Friday.
  4. Eat Meal 1 (lighter, higher carb) about 1.5 h before training and drink the Gatorade during.
  5. If a main lift drops 2+ sessions in a row, check sleep, pre-workout food and volume before touching calories.

Why your plan already protects muscle

  • Rate of loss: 0.5–0.8 kg/week is about 0.5–0.9% of body weight, inside the 0.5–1% range where strength usually holds.
  • Protein: 160–170 g is about 1.75–1.85 g/kg now and 2.0–2.1 g/kg at 80 kg, within the 1.6–2.2 g/kg evidence range for lifters in a deficit.
  • Calories: 2,000 kcal is a ~700–900 kcal deficit. Going lower (1,500–1,700) speeds loss but costs strength and recovery. Stay at 2,000.
  • Training: 3 years of lifting, 5–6 sessions a week, with every muscle hit twice per 8-day cycle. That frequency is enough to maintain and even slowly build.

Training rules while cutting

  • Keep loads heavy. Stay in your 8–12 rep range at the weights you use now. Don't switch to light, high-rep 'cutting' workouts.
  • Keep volume the same. Your current sets per exercise are enough. Adding sets during a deficit adds fatigue you can't recover from as well.
  • Train 1–2 reps short of failure on working sets. Going to failure on every set costs more recovery than it's worth on a cut.
  • Rest 2.5–3 min on big compounds (bench, incline DB, OHP, rows, pulldowns, hack squat). Short rests cost reps.
  • Double progression still applies: when you hit the top of the range on all sets, add weight. On a cut, matching last session is also a win.
  • Keep the treadmill as is: 30 min at ~4.0 km/h, incline ~3, after lifting. Low-intensity walking barely interferes with strength. Don't turn it into running or HIIT.

Fuel around training

  • Sun–Thu: Meal 1 + snacks at ~2 pm is your pre-workout. Make it the lighter, lower-fat, higher-carb meal; carbs are what you lift on.
  • Gatorade 1 L during the session gives ~60 g carbs (estimate until you send the label). It's inside the 2,000, so it's free performance.
  • Adonis at ~5 pm gives 22 g protein right after training. Meal 2 at 8–9 pm can be the heavier, higher-fat meal.
  • Saturday: allow 1.5–2 h after the eggs and khubz before training at 10 am.
  • Spread protein: Meal 1 (~60–70 g), Adonis (22 g), Meal 2 (~60–70 g). Three solid feedings a day is plenty.
  • Friday: the free day is fine. The Cajun shrimp still delivers 70–85 g protein. Sauce on the side keeps it from blowing up the day.

Recovery

  • Sleep is the biggest lever after protein. Aim for 7–9 hours; short sleep on a cut shows up as lost reps first. (Your usual bedtime is still an open item.)
  • Keep creatine at 5 g/day. It supports strength in a deficit. It holds 1–2 kg of water, which is stable and doesn't hide fat loss.
  • Keep steps steady at 6k–10k. Big swings in daily activity make the scale and your energy harder to read.
  • Keep hydration consistent: ~3 L rest days, ~3.5–4 L training days including the Gatorade.

When a lift drops

A drop is when the top working set on a main lift is lower than its best recent value (less weight, or fewer reps at the same weight) for 2 or more sessions of that lift in a row. A single bad session. Also ignore drops on the session right after a missed meal, a bad night, or Friday's free day.

Check these in order. Calories come last.

  1. 1

    Sleep

    Fewer than 7 hours on the nights before those sessions?

    Fix sleep for a week before changing anything else.

  2. 2

    Pre-workout food

    Was Meal 1 eaten ~1.5 h before, with carbs, and was the Gatorade drunk during?

    Pick a higher-carb Meal 1 and fruit snacks on training days.

  3. 3

    Volume and fatigue

    Did sets, failure training or session length creep up? Joints achy?

    Keep sets at baseline, stop 1–2 reps short, or take a deload week.

  4. 4

    Hydration and stress

    Low water, poor appetite, unusual stress?

    Bring fluids back to target and keep the routine steady.

  5. 5

    Calories (last)

    Is the 7-day average dropping faster than ~0.9 kg/week (1% of body weight) after the first 2 weeks?

    Only then add ~150–200 kcal of carbs to Meal 1 on training days. Not before the Nov 7 review.

What to expect

  • Weeks 1–6: most lifts usually hold or still go up. You're well fed relative to most cuts.
  • Later in the cut (roughly under 85 kg): a lift may lose 1–2 reps or stall. That's normal and doesn't mean you're losing muscle.
  • Pumps get smaller and you may feel flatter. That's glycogen and water, not muscle.
  • Bench and pressing may feel slightly harder as you get leaner (less leverage from body mass). Machine and isolation lifts usually hold best.

Tools for a long cut

  • Deload: if you feel beaten up for 1–2 weeks (joints, sleep, motivation), do one week with half the sets at the same weights, then resume.
  • Diet break (optional): after ~8–12 weeks of dieting, 1–2 weeks at maintenance (~2,700 kcal, mostly extra carbs) can restore training quality. Evidence is mixed; use it if lifts and energy are sliding, not on a schedule.
  • Don't change the program mid-cut unless something hurts. You decided on Oct 25 to keep it as is, and consistency is what lets you spot a real drop.

Things you don't need to do

  • Switch to high reps to 'tone' or 'cut'. Heavy work keeps muscle; light work doesn't burn meaningfully more fat.
  • Drop to 1,500–1,700 kcal because of one high weigh-in. Judge by the 7-day average.
  • Add extra cardio when the scale stalls for a few days. Wait for the weekly trend.
  • Take a shake 'just in case'. If the meals hit 160–170 g, you're covered.
